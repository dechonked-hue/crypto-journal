For years, one of the biggest arguments for ETH was simple. More network activity means more fees, more ETH gets burned, supply goes down, and price goes up.

Sounds great on paper. But Vitalik just pointed out why this narrative is flawed.

First, a lot of those massive fee burns came from speculation. NFT mania, meme coins, and other crypto hype cycles. When the hype dies, the fees disappear too. That's hardly a reliable foundation for long-term value.

Second, if Ethereum's value depends on burning massive amounts of ETH, then there's an incentive to keep transaction fees high. But shouldn't the goal be to make Ethereum cheaper, more accessible, and more useful?

Vitalik's argument is that Ethereum should focus on what actually matters: decentralization, censorship resistance, privacy, and security.

And I think he has a point.

**But there's still a question that needs answering.**

If Ethereum becomes the backbone of global finance, with stablecoins, tokenized assets, and trillions of dollars settling on-chain, how much of that value actually flows back to ETH?

People can use stablecoins without holding much ETH. Institutions can tokenize assets without needing to accumulate ETH.

And unlike Bitcoin, ETH doesn't have a fixed supply cap or a simple digital gold narrative.

So being useful doesn't automatically mean ETH will appreciate in value.

That said, I still believe ETH has a place in the future of finance.

Not because I expect transaction fees to remain ridiculously high, but because a truly decentralized, secure, and censorship-resistant settlement layer has value. ETH also plays a fundamental role in securing that network.

The question is whether that demand becomes significant enough to support ETH's value over time.

**My takeaway?**

I'm still bullish on ETH over the long term. But I think we need to be realistic about where its value comes from.

Ethereum becoming successful and ETH appreciating in value are two different things. Ideally, both happen. But one doesn't guarantee the other.

And that's probably the most important question surrounding ETH today.
