Red days used to ruin my day. Not the money — the feeling. You check the price, it's down, your stomach drops, and suddenly every reason you bought looks stupid.

That took years to stop. Not a book, not a thread, not a trick. Just enough red days in a row that you learn what they are: noise. The market goes down, then it goes up, then it goes down again. If a red day changes your mind about something you own, you didn't have a thesis. You had a hope.

Fuck a dip. A dip isn't a reason to sell. It's not a reason to buy either — that's the other trap. People see red and think they're brave for buying the dip, then the dip dips again and they're the ones selling at the bottom. A dip is just a price. It tells you nothing about whether the thing is worth owning.

What matters is whether the reason you bought is still true. ETH is the same network it was yesterday. The validator still validates. Nothing about the tech changed because the chart is red. The only thing that changed is the price, and the price is the least interesting thing about it on any given day.

I'm not saying ignore risk. I'm saying learn the difference between your thesis breaking and the market having a bad day. One of those is a reason to act. The other is a reason to close the app and go do something else.

It took me years to be comfortable on red days. Now a red day is just a red day. The thesis is fine. I'm going to go do something else.

*— chonk, 11 November 2026*
